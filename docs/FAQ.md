# Hookline FAQ

## What is Hookline?

Hookline is a multichain intelligence desk and API for onchain hooks. It helps people discover active hooks and projects, inspect deployed contracts, decode Uniswap v4 hook permissions, preserve evidence, compare deployments, and monitor supported networks.

## What problem does it solve?

Hook information is split across explorers, repositories, RPC calls, dashboards, and social posts. Hookline organizes it around one chain-aware identity and keeps direct observations separate from derived metrics and project claims.

## Where does the current data come from?

The board uses a timestamped v4.xyz community-indexer snapshot for hook discovery and aggregate pool and swap counts. Its directory metadata is community-curated, project-submitted, or Hookline-researched and labeled accordingly. Live contract evidence comes directly from configured Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base, Arbitrum One, and Robinhood Chain RPC endpoints. Hookline uses bytecode and call methods, current block height, deterministic hashing, and local permission decoding.

## Is the board exhaustive?

No. The sync collects activity leaders for every two-nibble hook-address prefix exposed by the public index. That creates broad cross-chain coverage without overstating completeness or overloading a community service. First-party PoolManager event indexing is the path to exhaustive, reproducible coverage.

## Why is the index snapshot older than the current time?

It records when the discovery snapshot was generated by the index-sync job, not when the page was opened. The deployed snapshot stays at that timestamp until another successful sync and release. Market quotes, direct contract observations, and project scans carry their own timestamps. The snapshot is not on an hourly auto-refresh promise.

## What is the Projects board?

A searchable view of hook-powered projects and infrastructure, separate from individual hook or token addresses. Source-linked deployments can share a project profile, pinned contract state, selected event history, and a Telegram follow. Listing breadth does not imply every project has a custom reader or complete history. Coverage and sources remain visible.

## How are Board, Observe, and Watchlists different?

Board is discovery across the indexed ecosystem. Observe is an on-demand contract inspection on a selected chain, including addresses absent from the index. Watchlists save selected contracts, successful observations, refresh history, and same-chain comparisons. Projects groups source-linked deployments under a mechanism or team; it does not replace those address-level views.

## Do CLAUS allocations prove buybacks or rewards happened?

No. Its source-bound reader separates configured fee allocations, accrued balances, buyback/burn events, liquidity events, and NFT reward events. Percentages state their basis and retain the raw parts-per-million value. For supported buyback/burn events, a successful transaction receipt must contain the exact CLAUS token transfer from the hook to the zero address before Hookline labels that transfer confirmed. Other events remain contract-reported. No event is presented as a lifetime total, return, or proof of economic benefit. ENGRAM owner fees remain accrued unless separate payout evidence supports a payment.

## Can teams submit projects, claim profiles, or suggest corrections?

Yes. Use the Projects form, `POST /api/project-submissions`, or the Telegram commands `/submitproject`, `/suggest`, and `/claim`. Suggestions enter private agent review. Claims use expiring DNS TXT challenges on a researched project domain. A successful claim proves domain control and permits metadata updates only, not edits to measured evidence or a safety endorsement. No email, payment, or HKLN holding is required. Private status receipts identify each request.

## Who handles review requests?

Automated validation handles limits and DNS verification. An authenticated scheduled agent reviews ordinary new listings and corrections against primary sources, recording the decision and proof. Routine requests do not become an owner email inbox. The reviewer runs on the configured workstation; requests remain pending while it is offline. Contract relationships and financial measurements require the separate evidence pipeline.

## What do project follows notify me about?

Selected changes observed on monitored contracts, such as implementation/configuration changes, supported factory launches, registry updates, and model/payment events. A rotating 10-minute scan uses bounded chain-specific windows. Profiles show actual source blocks, lag, and coverage. Baselines and historical catch-up are not sent as newly occurring activity. A log labeled payment is a recorded contract event, not a reconciliation of the entire project's payouts.

## Does every listed project have complete monitoring?

No. The Projects board includes researched and community records, linked deployments, and a smaller set of monitored targets. `linkedDeployments`, `monitoredDeployments`, and `observedDeployments` mean different things. A saved observation does not prove complete event history or current provider health. Profiles show the last successful read, scan status, and event lag.

## Does Hookline show pool volume and liquidity?

Yes. Open a hook profile to resolve related markets. When DexScreener has the pool, Hookline shows price, market capitalization, liquidity, 24-hour volume, and change while keeping the indexed hook relationship visible.

These are sourced market readings, not Hookline's own complete swap index. A missing value stays unavailable. Liquidity alerts compare the same indexed pool set with complete readings; a changed sample or missing data does not become a liquidity-loss alert.

## Does “Dynamic” show the fee a trade actually paid?

No. In Uniswap v4, the exact PoolKey value `0x800000` marks dynamic LP fees. It is not an 838.8608% fee. Hookline labels that configuration “Dynamic.” Static advertised LP fees, current dynamic LP fees, hook-level charges, and Hookline's execution fee are separate concepts. Current adaptive fees and extracted hook fees require swap-specific evidence and are not inferred from the flag.

## What do the 14 permission flags mean?

Uniswap v4 hook addresses encode enabled callback permissions in their low 14 bits. Hookline decodes those bits into the canonical callback names. An enabled callback says where the hook can participate in the pool lifecycle; it does not describe the full runtime behavior or determine safety.

## Does Hookline audit contracts?

No. Hookline provides evidence and monitoring. It is not a smart-contract audit, legal opinion, or trading recommendation.

## Why are comparisons limited to one chain?

An address is not a complete identity, and block heights on different chains do not share state or time semantics. Hookline requires the same chain for direct evidence comparisons.

## What happens when an RPC request fails?

The failure is shown explicitly. A failed refresh does not erase the most recent successful observation.

## Are watchlists uploaded?

Not automatically. Optional wallet sign-in lets you explicitly save list names, contract addresses, and trade preferences to a private account, then load them on another device. Raw local observations are not uploaded. Loading retains matching local history and backs up the previous device copy. Shared project observations and Telegram follows are stored separately so project monitoring can run without an open browser.

## What does signing in authorize?

A gas-free sign-in message establishes account identity only. It does not create a wallet, grant spending permission, connect a trading session, or place an order. Existing EOA wallets and supported deployed EIP-1271 wallets can sign in. Contract-wallet identities are bound to their original chain. Sessions last seven days and can be signed out individually or everywhere. Counterfactual wallet deployment is not performed.

## Can I create a wallet without installing MetaMask?

Yes. Open Wallets, sign in through an enabled Privy method, then explicitly choose Create my wallet. Google and email are enabled. The picker also supports external wallets, including MetaMask and mobile-wallet connections. Telegram and Apple login are not offered. The Telegram bot and alert subscriptions work independently of website login.

Return with the same login to reuse the same embedded wallet. Different unlinked login methods can create different accounts; they are not silently merged. Creating a wallet is optional for users bringing an existing one. Secure export opens Privy's own interface, not a Hookline key field. Never send a private key or seed phrase to the bot.

These are embedded EVM wallets, not smart-contract accounts. Each signature needs the user's confirmation. Gas sponsorship, delegated signing, and automatic orders are not enabled. Using a wallet does not automatically upload your research: account sync still requires a separate gas-free sign-in message and explicit Save or Load.

## Does linking Telegram merge my settings or wallets?

No. A one-use, expiring link connects the website account to the private Telegram identity. Existing Telegram alerts and settings stay intact; there is no silent settings merge or trading delegation. Sign-out preserves device data. Clear device copy removes local lists, observations, cached markets, backup, and trade preferences, not account data or Telegram alerts.

## Does Hookline submit transactions or connect my wallet?

Research remains anonymous and does not require a wallet. On supported EVM markets, Hookline can prepare a short-lived, fee-bound route for a connected browser wallet. The wallet signs and submits the exact reviewed transaction. Hookline's backend never signs, broadcasts, or receives wallet secrets, and the public RPC continues to reject transaction-submission and account-access methods.

## What is the paid RPC?

The paid endpoint uses x402 to request 0.01 USDC on Base for a higher-capacity Hookline request. A valid unpaid request receives a standard HTTP 402 challenge with the payment terms.

## What does the Telegram bot do?

Send it a token or hook address to navigate hook relationships, related tokens, markets, and trade previews. A website hook link opens the matching chain and profile directly. From a hook profile, enable a persistent alert for direct runtime changes, new indexed pool relationships, or indexed liquidity movement of 10% or more. Checks run every 10 minutes. Telegram does not sign; reviewed execution opens on the website for the user's wallet.

Alert cards show sourced project, token, or contract names when available, the chain, and the full contract address in a copyable code line. Details, Pause/Resume, Add alert, Projects, and Main menu buttons handle routine navigation. An unavailable name remains explicitly unnamed rather than inventing an affiliation.

## Can I save trading settings in Telegram?

Yes. Settings has buttons for slippage, native-coin buy amounts, and sell percentages. Telegram preferences persist privately for that Telegram user. A reviewed trade link carries the selected amount and slippage to the website; it never starts a quote or transaction automatically. Native presets are explicitly bound to native input, not silently interpreted as a stablecoin or wrapped-token amount. Percentage sells use the connected wallet's freshly read token balance.

Website preferences are saved on that device, with optional explicit Save and Load through the website account. They are not a synchronized copy of Telegram settings. A Telegram handoff overrides only the fields it explicitly supplies.

Priority-fee references and TP/SL profiles are saved configuration only. Priority fees remain wallet-managed. Hookline does not yet create custodial wallets, transfer or bridge funds through the bot, or place and monitor automatic limit, take-profit, stop-loss, or trailing-stop orders. No saved profile grants signing authority.

## Can Hookline use user-owned smart wallets?

Yes, that is the intended design for future automated orders, not a currently enabled wallet service. Users retain owner and recovery control. Automation would receive an explicitly approved, revocable, expiring permission restricted to approved chains, routers, assets, recipients, and spending limits. A restricted automation signer is not the owner's unrestricted key. Transfers, bridges, and owner changes require separate authority. Provider and chain support, recovery, revocation, and adversarial policy tests must be completed before launch.

## Does a token's Trade button guarantee execution through its displayed hook?

No. A reviewed aggregator quote may use other pools, and some custom hooks are not supported. The UI discloses that hook-specific routing is not verified. An unsupported quote must stop, not imply that an untested hook path works. A native-coin preset cannot silently become a WETH or stablecoin amount.

## What does Hookline execution cost?

Hookline shows a 1% gross execution fee and applies 0.3% cashback instantly in the route, leaving a 0.7% effective fee. There is no deferred cashback claim. A short-lived intent binds the wallet, chain, destination, calldata, value, and economics before signing. After confirmation, Hookline independently verifies the transaction and stores a compact receipt without keys, signatures, or signed transaction material.

## Do I need a Hookline account?

No. Discovery, inspection, local watchlists, and alerts do not require a Hookline login. Telegram supplies its own identity for alert subscriptions. Optional wallet-message sign-in enables private list/settings save and load across devices; neither connecting nor creating a wallet silently uploads anything.

## Does market capitalization create revenue for Hookline?

No. Market capitalization itself does not create creator fees. Venue fees depend on actual trading activity, and product revenue depends on people paying for useful services such as capacity, history, alerts, team workflows, or integrations.

## Is Hookline affiliated with Uniswap, Robinhood, Flaunch, or PayAI?

No. Hookline is independent and is not endorsed by those organizations. It uses public protocols, chains, tools, and infrastructure where documented.

## How can builders contribute?

Open a focused issue with the chain, contract, block range, expected result, and reproduction steps. Code contributions should include tests and preserve Hookline's evidence and safety constraints.

## What comes next?

Deepen the shared project readers, improve event coverage and freshness, then connect official PoolManager activity to measured fees and outcomes. Expand the reusable execution controls separately, with user-owned smart-account automation only after its signing and order-lifecycle checks pass. Alerts are selected for evidence, not expected trading fees.
