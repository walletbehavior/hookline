## What changed

The Projects board starts with 39 ecosystem records. Sixteen researched seed profiles supply 35 source-linked deployment references, merged with the existing community records. The scope includes Clanker, Zora, Doppler, Flaunch, Pons, Angstrom, Alphix, Bunni, Arrakis, EulerSwap, OpenZeppelin, Uniswap Labs, CLAUS, ENGRAM, and other hook-related projects. Inclusion is not an affiliation, endorsement, or claim that every record has a current v4 deployment.

Ten projects have 15 explicitly selected monitoring targets. The public API separates linked deployments, configured monitoring targets, and successful observations. The shared reader system covers pinned state and selected mechanism events. Clanker, Pons, Zora, and ENGRAM have source-bound specialized readers; generic runtime, owner, implementation, and supported event reads serve other targets. Configuration is not activity, and activity is not a reconciled outcome.

Projects, deployments, observations, events, and subscriptions share a schema. Each retained observation identifies its source block and probe status. Contract-event records retain transaction/log evidence. Public activity excludes orphaned records; baselines and historical catch-up do not become fresh-activity notifications.

Suggestions, corrections, and domain claims use private API receipts. DNS TXT proof grants metadata control only. Ordinary requests go to an authenticated agent review queue instead of an email inbox. The scheduled reviewer depends on its configured workstation being online. No payment or HKLN holding is required to submit.

The Telegram runtime adds named alert cards, full copyable contract addresses, and Details/Pause/Resume/Add alert/Projects navigation. Saved trade preferences include slippage, native-coin amounts, sell percentages, and inactive TP/SL profiles. The website has compact settings, device-local preferences, exact balance-percent sizing, and explicit wallet handoffs. No link quotes, signs, or submits on navigation.

The board's index timestamp is smaller and located below the Hook / Token / Project selector. It remains the actual snapshot-generation time, not a page-refresh clock. Dynamic-fee sentinels are displayed as Dynamic instead of misformatted percentages. Indexed-liquidity alerts require complete measurements of the same pool set.

## Operational boundaries

- The scanner rotates at most six targets per 10-minute run, with bounded RPC, event, page, time, and retention limits. Each target is not promised a fresh read every 10 minutes.
- Public RPC throttling or missing historical data can delay a read or event cursor. Last-good evidence remains available, with scan status and lag shown. A supported chain is not a provider availability guarantee.
- Community discovery snapshots are refreshed by a successful sync and release, not automatically every hour.
- Market quotes come from external market sources; first-party full swap indexing is not shipped.
- Runtime equality is not proof of common ownership, identical configuration, or a new launch.
- Pool-advertised configuration is not the fee actually extracted. Reconciled buybacks, burns, rewards, fee attribution, and refusal rates remain reader/Hook Tape work.
- Aggregator quotes can use other pools. Hook-specific execution is not certified merely because the market is displayed.
- Smart-wallet creation, bridging, transfers, automated limit orders, and active TP/SL are not enabled. Saved profiles grant no signing permission. Priority references do not override wallet fees.
- Trading volume and expected execution revenue do not select project evidence or rank change alerts.

## Validation

Production checks on October 7 confirmed all 15 selected contracts across 10 projects had at least one saved observation, the named/full-address alert card was delivered by `@HooklineTradeBot`, the configured webhook pointed at Hookline, and the updated FAQ was visible on the live site. This is a point-in-time rollout check, not a claim that every event cursor is current or every route can execute.

The release includes worker regression checks, 37 real-SQLite evidence tests, 24 contribution tests, 20 trading-preference tests, 10 Projects API/Telegram integration tests, 52 bot tests, and focused Projects, trade-preference, reader, and dynamic-fee UI suites. These tests exercise permissions, private identity separation, canonical block checks, preserved reads, idempotent delivery, exact amounts, and unsupported-path rejection. Passing tests are not an audit or proof of complete market coverage.
